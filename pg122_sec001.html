A proper house for pigs should have the following features:

(a) Ability to protect pigs: A house for pig should be well constructed using

durable materials to protect the animals from adverse weather conditions

(rain, sun,wind) and protect them from theft and predators.

(b) Adequate space: To allow pigs to move freely and access feed and water

without crowding. It should have a separate area (pens) for different stages

of growth.

(c) Well-ventilated: To ensure a continuous supply of fresh air and remove

excess heat, moisture, and harmful gases (e.g., ammonia).

(d) Facilitate easy cleanliness and hygiene: The floors, walls, and equipment

should be easy to clean and disinfect to maintain high hygiene standards.

(e) Adequate lighting: Natural light through windows or skylights to support

pigs’ circadian rhythms. Alternatively, artificial lighting can be supplemented

to ensure the proper light levels, especially in darker environments.

(f) Feed systems should be designed to minimise feed wastage and ensure all

pigs have access to feed.

(g) Bedding and comfort: Use bedding materials (e.g., straw, sawdust) to provide

comfort, absorb moisture, and reduce stress. Proper insulation and bedding

help pigs regulate body temperature and stay warm.

(h) Biosecurity measures: Access to the piggery should be restricted to prevent

disease introduction. Footbaths, hand sanitizers, and disinfection points

should be available to maintain cleanliness.

(i) Emergency preparedness: Fire alarms and safety equipment should be

installed to manage fire risks. Clear emergency plans, including those for

health crises, natural disasters, and power outages, should be installed on

the walls.

(j) Efficiency and sustainability: Use resources (feed, water, energy) efficiently

to reduce costs and environmental impact. Implement waste recycling or

composting systems to utilise manure and minimise ecological impact.
