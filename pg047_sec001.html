(f) Marketing: Understanding market aspects can help banana stakeholders

benefit from opportunities in the banana production industry. Some of

these marketing aspects are as follows:

(i) Branding and packaging: Develop a strong brand and use durable

packaging that attract buyers.

(ii) Market segmentation: Identify and target different market segments,

that is local market such as rural/urban markets, restaurants, and

supermarkets; and export markets. It is important to plan and design

an approach to meet the specific needs of each segment. Moreover,

sanitary and phytosanitary standards and measures for the crop

produce must be taken into consideration especially when targeting

export markets.

(iii) Quality assurance: Because different markets have different quality

standards, it is advisable to implement proper post-harvest handling

practices, such as cleaning, sorting, and packaging.

(iv) Partnerships: Partner with local retailers, supermarkets, and

restaurants to supply them with bananas. Establishing long-term

contracts can ensure a steady demand.

(v) Promotions and discounts: To attract more customers, run promotions,

offer discounts, or create bundles. For example, offer a discount on

bulk purchases or during special events.

(vi) Value-added products: Diversify your product range by offering

value-added banana products such as banana chips, dried bananas,

or banana-based snacks and desserts to capture more customers.

(vii) Customer engagement: Engage with customers through social media.

Post banana images on your page, including details such as location

and selling price, newsletters, and in-store events. Share recipes,

health benefits of bananas, and updates about your farm or business.

(viii) Seasonal strategies: Capitalise on the peak banana seasons with

targeted marketing campaigns and ensure consistent supply

throughout the year to maintain customer trust.
