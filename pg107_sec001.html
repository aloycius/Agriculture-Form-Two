Livestock breeding systems

In livestock breeding, the animals with desired characteristics are selected and

allowed to mate. In this way, the desirable genes from these animals are inherited,

that is, transmitted to the offspring, making it perform similarly or even better

than the parents. Proper livestock breeding ensures the continuity improvement

of farm animal generation after generation. Thus, the main objectives of livestock

breeding are to:

(a) Obtain high-quality animal products, e.g., milk, meat, wool, and eggs.

(b) Increase the production capacity of the animal in terms of quantity, such as

milk yield, growth rate, weight gain, and number of eggs.

(c) Increase the tolerance of an animal against diseases.

(d) Increase animal adaptability to the environment.

Factors to consider in selecting an animal for breeding

For a breeding system to give good results, it has to go together with proper

selection; hence, selection and breeding systems are also termed as tools for

improving livestock’s genetic makeup. Using these tools, different animal traits

are identified, selected, and measured, and the best ones are used as parent

animals. In the selection process, specific individuals are chosen to be the next

generation’s parents based on their desirable characteristics. The principle is to

‘keep the best and cull the poorest.’ On farms, various factors are considered

when identifying the individuals to be selected, mainly based on observable and

measurable traits. The factors include:

(a) Age of the animal: Young animals are given priority because they have

longer productive lives than old ones, and breeding efficiency declines

with age.

(b) Performance level: Animals with the highest production records should

be selected first.
